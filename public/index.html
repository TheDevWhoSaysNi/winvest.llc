Reg CF, Reg A+, and Reg D

Let your customers become your investors.

Most startup raises we support use one of three U.S. paths: Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF, up to $5 million), Regulation A+ (Tier 2 up to $75 million), or Regulation D (Rules 506(b) and 506(c), no set cap). We help you pick the one that fits, build an offering investors trust, and run the campaign that gets it funded.

Regulation Crowdfunding

Reg CF

Up to $5M per 12 months

Raise from anyone, customers and fans included, through an SEC-registered funding portal or broker-dealer. This is equity crowdfunding for startups: a securities offering, not a rewards campaign.

Great forEarly startups, consumer brands, restaurants, local businesses, and community-backed companies.

Regulation A+

Reg A+

Up to $75M Tier 2, per 12 months

A public offering the general public can join once the SEC qualifies it. Tier 1 goes to $20 million. Tier 2 goes to $75 million and needs audited financials. More preparation, a much bigger ceiling than Reg CF.

Great forGrowth-stage startups with traction and a story ready for a wide audience.

Regulation D

Reg D

No set cap Rule 506(b) or 506(c)

A private placement. Rule 506(b) stays with people you already know and can include up to 35 non-accredited investors who meet a sophistication standard. Rule 506(c) can be advertised, and every buyer must be a verified accredited investor.

Great forStartups raising from angels, family offices, and private networks.

FAQ

Do I have to be raising money to work with you?

Not at all. Plenty of startups come to us for a website, IT help, ads, or a sales process before they are ready to raise. Others hire us for a Reg CF, Reg A+, or Reg D round. Capital raising is one of the things we do, not a requirement.

Are you a broker-dealer or funding portal?

No. Winvest is a consulting and services firm. We are not a broker-dealer, funding portal, investment adviser, or law firm, and we do not offer or sell securities. When an offering requires an SEC-registered funding portal, broker-dealer, or securities attorney, we work alongside them and help you find the right fit.

Which raise is right for me: Reg CF, A+, or D?

It depends on how much you need, who you want to raise from, and how ready the company is. Reg CF fits community-backed startup raises up to $5 million in 12 months. Reg A+ fits larger public raises, up to $20 million on Tier 1 and $75 million on Tier 2. Reg D, under Rule 506(b) or 506(c), fits private rounds and has no set dollar cap. We’ll walk you through it on a free call.

What do you do on a Reg CF, Reg A+, or Reg D raise?

We prepare the company to raise and we market the offering: pitch deck, campaign page, video, financial story, ads, email, and investor updates. Securities counsel prepares the legal documents. An SEC-registered funding portal or broker-dealer conducts the offering when the rules require one. Winvest is the operating partner alongside them, not the intermediary.

Is Regulation Crowdfunding the same as rewards crowdfunding?

No. Rewards campaigns, such as Kickstarter, trade perks for money and do not sell securities. Regulation Crowdfunding, often called equity crowdfunding or Reg CF, does sell securities. A Reg CF raise is currently capped at $5 million in a 12-month period and has to run through an SEC-registered funding portal or broker-dealer. Non-accredited investors have personal investment limits based on income and net worth. Accredited investors do not.

What is the difference between Reg A+ Tier 1 and Tier 2?

Both can be offered to the public after the SEC qualifies the offering. Tier 1 is currently limited to $20 million in a 12-month period and still goes through state review. Tier 2 is currently limited to $75 million, requires audited financials and ongoing SEC reports, and is the Reg A+ path most growth-stage startups mean. On Tier 2, a non-accredited investor is generally limited to 10 percent of the greater of income or net worth.

What is the difference between Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c)?

Both are Regulation D exemptions, and neither has a set dollar cap. Rule 506(b) cannot be publicly advertised. It is for people the company already knows, and it allows up to 35 non-accredited investors who meet a sophistication standard. Rule 506(c) can be advertised, but every purchaser has to be an accredited investor and the company has to take reasonable steps to verify that.
