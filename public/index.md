## Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF)

- Often called equity crowdfunding. It sells securities. A rewards campaign, such as Kickstarter, trades perks for money and does not.
- Current issuer limit: $5 million in a 12-month period.
- Open to the general public, including customers and fans.
- Must be conducted through an SEC-registered funding portal or broker-dealer, and only on that intermediary’s platform.
- Non-accredited investors have personal investment limits based on income and net worth. Accredited investors do not have that Reg CF cap.
- A fit for early startups, consumer brands, restaurants, local businesses, and community-backed companies.

## Regulation A+ (Reg A)

- A public offering the general public can join after the SEC qualifies it. Qualification is required. Filing alone is not enough.
- Tier 1: currently up to $20 million in a 12-month period, with state review.
- Tier 2: currently up to $75 million in a 12-month period. Requires audited financials and ongoing SEC reports. This is the path most growth-stage companies mean by Reg A+.
- On Tier 2, a non-accredited investor is generally limited to 10 percent of the greater of income or net worth (or, for an entity, revenue or net assets). Accredited investors are not subject to that cap.
- Affiliate selling securityholders have lower sub-limits than the headline ceiling.
- A fit for growth-stage startups with traction and a story ready for a wide audience.
- GolfSuites, a Tampa Bay golf-entertainment company, raised $57 million across multiple Regulation A+ offerings. Winvest helped take that story to everyday investors. Venues funded include locations in Texas, Louisiana, and Alabama. Past results do not guarantee future outcomes.

## Regulation D

- Private placements, most often Rule 506(b) or Rule 506(c). Neither rule sets a dollar cap. Rule 504 is a different, smaller exemption and is not the path described on the site.
- Rule 506(b) cannot be publicly advertised. It is for people the company already knows. It allows up to 35 non-accredited investors who meet a sophistication standard, plus unlimited accredited investors.
- Rule 506(c) can be advertised. Every purchaser must be an accredited investor, and the company must take reasonable steps to verify that status.
- A fit for startups raising from angels, family offices, and private networks.
- Paying someone transaction-based compensation to solicit investors generally requires a registered broker-dealer. Winvest does not act as that broker-dealer.
